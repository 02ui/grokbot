# Onboarding (read this when the user wants to set up their brain)

This is a one-time setup. Your job is to interview the user and then write three files: `who-you-are.md`, `what-you-do.md`, and `what-you-want.md`. When you are done, all three should be filled in with their real answers.

Run this when the user says "help me set up my brain", "run onboarding", or agrees to your first-run offer.

## How to run it

Keep it human. You are having a short conversation, not filling in a form.

- **Ask in small batches**, not all at once. Two or three questions, wait for the answer, then continue.
- **Plain language.** Assume the person has no technical background unless they tell you otherwise.
- **Everything is skippable.** If someone says "skip" or "not sure", move on and leave that part blank.
- **Do not interrogate.** If an answer covers two questions, do not re-ask.
- **Reflect back.** After each batch, briefly confirm what you heard so they can correct you.
- Aim for **5 minutes**, not 30.

## What to find out

### Part 1 — Who they are (fills `who-you-are.md`)

1. **Name and what they do.** "What should I call you, and what do you do?"
2. **Where they are based.** Optional. Only useful for timezone or local context.
3. **Technical level.** "Do you write code, or should I keep everything non-technical?"
4. **How they want you to work.** "Short and direct, or more explanation? Patient or fast?"

### Part 2 — What they do (fills `what-you-do.md`)

5. **Their work.** "What do you spend most of your time on?"
6. **Skills and tools.** "What are you good at? What tools do you use every day?"
7. **Current focus.** "What is actually on your plate this week or month?"

### Part 3 — What they want (fills `what-you-want.md`)

8. **Short-term goals.** "Anything you want to finish or start soon?"
9. **Longer-term direction.** "Where do you want to be in a year?"
10. **What success looks like.** "When this brain is working well, what changes for you?"

## When you are done

1. Write their answers into `who-you-are.md`, `what-you-do.md`, and `what-you-want.md`, replacing the placeholder content.
2. Update `brain-content.js` so the HTML index shows the new text.
3. Show the user all three finished files and ask: *"Does this sound like you? Change anything that is off."*
4. Make any corrections they ask for.
5. Tell them they are set up, and that they can change these files any time — just edit the .md files or tell you.

Keep the writing in their own words. These files should not sound like generic AI output.
